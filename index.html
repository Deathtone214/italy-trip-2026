Airports to city

  • Rome Fiumicino (FCO): Leonardo Express to Termini (~32 min, ~) or fixed-fare taxi (~).
  • Milan Malpensa (MXP): Malpensa Express to Centrale or Cadorna (~50 min).
  • Milan Linate (LIN): M4 metro (~20 min to the centre).

Local transport

  • Rome: Metro A/B, buses, lots of walking.
  • Bologna: compact and walkable.
  • Venice: vaporetto (~ single; day passes available). No cars, no wheels on the bridges, so pack light.
  • Milan: M1/M2/M3 metro and trams.

Tickets

  • High-speed tickets are seat-assigned. Advance fares are cheaper.
  • Regional trains: validate the paper ticket at the green/yellow machine before boarding, or risk a fine.
  • Check the platform (binario) on the departure board about 15 minutes before.
  • Use Venezia Santa Lucia, not Mestre.

Best Times & Options by Time of Day

When to move, when to sightsee, and how to get around at each hour.

Early morning · 6:30–9:00

Best for day-trip trains (Florence/Pisa, Venice) and for beating the crowds at the Colosseum, Trevi and St. Mark's. High-speed trains are frequent; regional trains are the cheapest. Taxis are scarce, so pre-book.

Midday · 10:00–15:00

Best for city-to-city transfers (Rome → Bologna, Bologna → Milan): calm stations and an easy afternoon check-in. Attractions are busiest, so plan indoor sights and a leisurely lunch.

Late afternoon · 15:00–19:00

Great for rooftops and viewpoints (Duomo terraces, Florence, Pisa) in golden light. Return trains from day trips leave around now; commuter crowds build after 5 PM.

Evening · 19:00 onward

Best for Trevi, Navigli aperitivo and Bologna's porticoes. Late regional trains run until about 9–11 PM; metros close around midnight (Milan) or 11:30 PM (Rome). Use licensed taxis or a taxi app.

October at a glance

  • Typical highs are about 60s–70s °F (15–23 °C) with cooler mornings. Bring layers and a light rain jacket; autumn showers are common.
  • Venice can flood at high tide (acqua alta) in autumn. Bring waterproof shoes and check the alert app.
  • Italy is on daylight time (UTC+2) until Oct 25, 2026, which is 6–8 hours ahead of US time zones; expect some jet lag on day one.
  • Lunch is roughly 12:30–2:30 PM and dinner rarely starts before 7:30 PM. Some shops close mid-afternoon; churches often close 12–3 PM.

Notes & Travel Requirements

Documents, border rules and practical tips. Rules change; confirm with the official sources linked below before you fly.

Which passport? This section covers a US passport holder first. If you travel on a different passport, or are a US green-card or visa holder, entry rules for Italy and re-entry rules for the US are different. See the "other status" boxes below.

Entering Italy (Schengen area)

Passport

  • Must be valid for at least 3 months after you leave the Schengen area (through Jan 19, 2027 at minimum). 6 months is safer.
  • Issued within the last 10 years, with blank pages.
  • US citizens: no visa for stays up to 90 days in any 180-day period.
  • Carry a copy of the photo page, stored separately.

EES biometric registration

  • The EU's Entry/Exit System registers non-EU visitors (fingerprints and photo) at first entry and replaces passport stamping.
  • It began rolling out in Oct 2025 and was scheduled to be fully in place by April 2026, so expect a short registration step at your first Schengen border, possibly a kiosk.
  • Allow extra time on arrival, especially at busy airports.

ETIAS

  • The EU's ETIAS travel authorisation (about €20) has been repeatedly delayed. Sources I found say it is not yet required, with launch expected late 2026 or 2027.
  • Verify before you go on the official EU site. Only apply through official EU pages; look-alike sites charge extra.

At the border

  • Have your hotel bookings, onward/return ticket and proof of funds handy (rarely asked, but allowed).
  • Fill in nothing online for Italy; there is no arrival card.
  • Cash above €10,000 must be declared on entry or exit.
Other status (green card, visa, other passport): carry your passport and your green card (Form I-551) or valid US visa. Check whether your passport needs a Schengen visa at travel-europe.europa.eu, and confirm with the Italian consulate if unsure.

Returning to the United States (CBP)

Documents

  • US citizens: a valid US passport (a Real ID driver's licence is not enough for international flights).
  • Green-card holders: passport plus green card. Absences over 6 months can raise questions about residency.
  • Visa holders: passport, valid visa and your I-94 status; check that your status permits re-entry.

Faster processing

  • Mobile Passport Control (free app) can speed up the line at many airports.
  • Global Entry (paid, background check and interview) uses kiosks. Apply well in advance if you want it for future trips.
  • Keep the airline's flight number and your US address handy.
CBP: returning

Customs allowances

  • Personal duty-free exemption is typically $800 per person if you've been abroad 48 hours or more (limits on alcohol and tobacco apply).
  • Alcohol: about 1 litre duty-free, age 21+. Tobacco: about 200 cigarettes.
  • Declare cash or monetary instruments of $10,000 or more.
  • Keep receipts for gifts and purchases.

Food to bring home

  • You must declare all food, even if it turns out to be allowed.
  • Meat and pork products (cured meats such as mortadella and prosciutto) are generally restricted. Fresh produce, plants and some dairy are also restricted.
  • Sealed hard cheeses, olive oil, vinegar, dried pasta and chocolate are usually fine. Check CBP's guidance before buying.

Before you leave

  • Enrol in the free Smart Traveler Enrollment Program (STEP) so the US Embassy can reach you in an emergency.
  • Buy travel insurance with medical cover; US health plans usually do not cover Italy.
  • Tell your bank and card issuers about your dates. Carry two cards and some cash.
  • Check that your phone plan has roaming or buy an eSIM.
  • Emergencies: dial 112 in Italy.
  • US Embassy Rome: Via Vittorio Veneto 121, Rome. Map
  • Power: 230 V with Type F/L plugs. Bring an adapter (most US chargers are dual-voltage).
  • Medication: carry it in original packaging with a doctor's letter for prescriptions.
  • Book ahead: Colosseum (name-matched tickets), Duomo rooftop, Last Supper, rail tickets.
  • Churches require covered shoulders and knees.
  • Hotels collect a city tax per person per night (roughly ), paid at the desk and not included in online prices.
  • Watch for pickpockets at stations and on crowded metros; keep your passport in a hotel safe and carry a copy.
  • Tap water is safe; a restaurant "coperto" (cover charge) is normal and tipping is modest.

Priority experiences

Rome: Colosseum, Forum, Trevi at night, carbonara · Bologna: porticoes, Piazza Maggiore, Two Towers, tagliatelle al ragù · Florence: Duomo, Ponte Vecchio, bistecca · Pisa: Leaning Tower · Venice: St. Mark's, Rialto, vaporetto, cicchetti · Milan: Duomo rooftop, Galleria, Last Supper, Navigli · Lake Como: lakefront, funicular, ferries.
